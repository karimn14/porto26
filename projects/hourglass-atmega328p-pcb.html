Executive Summary: Designed a two-board, hourglass-shaped electronic sand timer driven by an ATmega8A microcontroller. Controls 29 high-brightness blue LEDs arranged in opposing triangular sand chambers through a cascaded chain of four 74HC595 shift registers over hardware SPI. Dual SW-200D tilt ball switches detect board inversion, triggering gravity-accurate falling sand animations, selectable 1/5/10-minute intervals, and active buzzer alarms.

01Project Concept & Mechanical Geometry

Traditional mechanical sand timers are aesthetically captivating but lack precision, duration flexibility, and audio alerts. The goal was to design an electronic hourglass PCB that replicates the physics of flowing sand grains using an animated LED matrix in a novel hourglass-shaped board contour.

The design utilizes a two-board stacked architecture: the front board displays the 29-LED sand matrix and orientation tilt switches, while the rear board houses the ATmega8A controller, duration selection buttons, buzzer alarm, and LiPo charging circuitry.

Fig 1. Figure 1: Complete circuit schematic: ATmega8A MCU, four 74HC595 shift registers in SPI cascade, dual SW-200D tilt switches, and TP4056 LiPo charger.

02Cascaded 74HC595 SPI Matrix & Shared Waist LED

Driving 29 LEDs with an ATmega8A (DIP-28) without exhausting IO lines required an optimized multiplexing architecture:

  • Cascaded Shift Register Chain: Connected four 8-bit 74HC595 shift registers in a daisy chain using just three microcontroller pins (SPI MOSI, SCK, and Latch). This yields 32 controllable digital outputs, with 29 allocated to LEDs and 3 for expansion.
  • Shared Center Waist LED: In Version 4.0, the two center LEDs from earlier prototypes were merged into a single shared LED located exactly at the hourglass waist. This represents the bottleneck through which sand grains fall, and reduced the required inter-board connector from 8 to 7 pins.
  • Dual SW-200D Tilt Switches: Two ball tilt switches mounted at 45-degree opposing angles detect physical board inversion, triggering a reverse-gravity sand flow sequence.
Fig 2. Figure 2: Pin mapping, shift register bit allocation table, and state machine transition flow (Idle, Running, Sparkle, Alarm).

03Power Management & State Machine Firmware

Circuit design and firmware state sequencer:

  • Power Supply & Battery Charging: Powered by a rechargeable 3.7V LiPo battery managed by an onboard TP4056 linear charger with micro-USB input, stepped down to a steady 3.3V logic rail via an AMS1117-3.3 regulator.
  • Selectable Durations: Three tactile buttons on the back PCB allow users to select 1-minute, 5-minute, or 10-minute timer intervals, confirmed with distinct audio chirps from an active buzzer.
  • Animation State Machine: The ATmega8A firmware (programmed via ISP using MiniCore) implements a non-blocking state machine with four operational phases: Idle Breathing, Running Sand Flow with pseudo-random grain drops, Done Sparkle animation, and Buzzer Alarm.

04Visual Flow & Electrical Validation

Validation across breadboard and initial board fabrication:

  • Achieved smooth 60 Hz LED refresh rates over hardware SPI with zero visible flicker or brightness variance.
  • Accurate timing countdown within 0.5 seconds of nominal time across 1, 5, and 10-minute cycles.
  • Current consumption during sleep mode measured under 15 µA, enabling months of standby battery shelf life.
