Executive Summary: Built an intelligent temperature-controlled food delivery box engineered around a dual-core ESP32 running FreeRTOS. Core 0 executes closed-loop PID thermal control holding stable setpoints of 23°C (cold storage via TEC Peltier) and 45°C (hot delivery via PTC air heating), while Core 1 handles wireless telemetry and mobile app communication. Designed a custom Power Distribution Board (PDB) featuring INA219 battery monitoring, BMS protection, and MOSFET load switches.

01Project Context & Thermal Challenge

Maintaining hot meals above the food-safety danger zone (>60°C or regulated 45°C holding) and cold groceries below 23°C during long motorcycle delivery routes in tropical climates requires active climate regulation. Passive insulated bags degrade within 30 minutes, resulting in food spoilage or compromised quality.

I engineered an active climate-regulated container featuring independent heating and cooling loops, robust Li-ion battery management with supplementary solar input, and real-time smartphone telemetry.

02Dual-Core FreeRTOS Firmware & Control Pipeline

The ESP32 firmware leverages symmetric multiprocessing (SMP) across both Xtensa LX6 cores:

  • Core 0 (Thermal Control Task): Dedicated exclusively to reading calibrated DHT22 / DS18B20 digital sensors at 1 Hz, executing a PID anti-windup algorithm, and outputting PWM to gate high-current MOSFET switches controlling the 12V 60W PTC heater and TEC1-12706 Peltier cooler.
  • Core 1 (IoT & Network Task): Manages Bluetooth LE and Wi-Fi connections, serving an asynchronous WebSockets interface to a custom Android mobile application built with Kodular. Transmits live chamber temperature, humidity, battery percentage, and solar charge status.
  • Hardware Watchdog Timer: Configured a hardware watchdog that automatically resets the microcontroller if either core deadlocks, safely de-energizing heating elements to prevent thermal runaway.
Fig 2. Figure 2: Power Distribution Board (PDB) schematic: 3S Li-ion battery management, INA219 current monitoring, MP1584 buck regulators, and MOSFET drivers.

03Custom Power Distribution Board (PDB) Schematic

To power both digital logic and high-draw thermal elements safely, I designed a dedicated PDB:

  • Battery Management & Monitoring: Powered by a 3S 12.6V Li-ion battery pack with an integrated hardware BMS circuit for over-current, short-circuit, and undervoltage cutoff. Integrated an INA219 high-side I2C current/voltage sensor to track power consumption and state-of-charge.
  • Regulated Power Rails: Stepped down 12V raw battery power to 5V (using a high-efficiency MP1584 buck converter) for logic and cooling fans, and 3.3V for the ESP32.
  • Logic-Level MOSFET Load Switching: Implemented low-resistance IRLZ44N N-channel MOSFETs with optocoupler gate drivers and flyback protection diodes to switch inductive blower fans and resistive heating elements without injecting transients into the digital ground.

04Thermal Regulation & Battery Life Performance

Empirical testing inside the 40 × 30 × 27 cm insulated enclosure verified strong thermal performance:

  • Maintained steady-state temperature within ±0.5 °C of target setpoints (23 °C cooling and 45 °C warming) under 34 °C ambient tropical sunlight.
  • Achieved over 3.5 hours of continuous active regulation on a single battery charge, extended by 45 minutes with the top-mounted solar auxiliary panel.
  • Seamless mobile telemetry updating every 1.0 second with remote target setpoint adjustments.
