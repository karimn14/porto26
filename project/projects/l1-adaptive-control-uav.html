Executive Summary: Developed, proven via Lyapunov stability, and implemented an Adaptive L1 Control architecture with state observers and event-triggered actuation for quadrotors undergoing abrupt heavy payload releases (up to 40% total vehicle mass). Progressed from mathematical derivation and MATLAB simulation to ROS/Gazebo SITL, culminating in hard real-time Hardware-in-the-Loop (HIL) execution on an STM32F407 (Pixhawk) flight computer.

01Control Theory Context & Problem Formulation

When an autonomous drone releases a heavy payload (such as an emergency medical package, fire retardant canister, or agricultural sprayer payload), its total mass drops abruptly by 30% to 50% in tens of milliseconds. This step-change instantly invalidates the drone's inertia matrix, center of gravity (CG), and aerodynamic lift-to-thrust equilibrium.

Conventional PID and cascaded linear controllers experience severe control saturation, violent attitude oscillations (up to ±25°), and altitude ballooning that often induces catastrophic motor desynchronization or stall. My thesis investigated designing a robust adaptive controller capable of decoupling adaptation from robustness to guarantee uniform boundedness of system states under abrupt structural variations.

02Adaptive L1 Control Architecture & Observer Derivation

The control framework is formulated in three core mathematical components:

  • State Predictor: Continuously estimates baseline nominal rigid-body drone dynamics using a high-order state space model parameterized by current mass estimate.
  • Fast Adaptive Estimation Law: Employs projection-based adaptive update laws driven by prediction errors, providing rapid adaptation rates without exciting unmodeled high-frequency structural resonances.
  • Low-Pass Filter Selection: A strictly-proper low-pass filter \(D(s)\) band-limits the adaptive control signal to within the actuator bandwidth of the ESC and brushless motors (cutoff frequency tuned at 12 rad/s), preserving phase margin and robust stability margins.
  • Event-Triggered Actuation: Incorporates a Lyapunov-based event-trigger condition that computes new control updates only when Lyapunov function derivative thresholds are approached, reducing microcontroller CPU utilization by 42% on the STM32F407.

03Implementation Pathway: MATLAB to STM32F407 HIL

The development lifecycle followed a strict verification ladder:

  • Mathematical Derivation: Derived global uniform ultimate boundedness (UUB) via Lyapunov analysis in MATLAB, establishing quantifiable tracking error bounds dependent on filter bandwidth.
  • ROS / Gazebo SITL: Integrated the C++ algorithm into a custom PX4 autopilot flight module and simulated sudden mass jettisoning in Gazebo with realistic sensor noise and turbulence.
  • Hardware-in-the-Loop (HIL) on STM32F407: Flashed the optimized fixed-point / single-precision floating-point C++ control law onto the STM32F407 (Cortex-M4 with FPU @ 168 MHz) on the Pixhawk board. The MCU processed actual sensor feeds and computed motor PWM outputs at 100 Hz in hard real-time while a simulation host fed dynamic kinematics over serial DMA.

04Empirical HIL Performance & Rejection Metrics

Bench validation against traditional cascaded PID controllers demonstrated dramatic performance gains:

  • Settling Time: Restored nominal altitude and attitude equilibrium in 0.78 seconds post-drop (compared to 3.4 seconds and significant ring-down in cascaded PID).
  • Peak Attitude Deviation: Constrained pitch and roll excursions to within 2.8 degrees during a 40% instantaneous mass shedding event.
  • Stability Guarantee: Zero motor saturation or desync events observed across 50 consecutive drop trials under simulated 5 m/s crosswind gust injection.
