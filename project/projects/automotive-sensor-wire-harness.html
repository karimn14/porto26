Executive Summary: Contributed to the engineering development and manufacturing documentation of a Rev. 0 prototype automotive sensor wire harness during an R&D internship at PT Hana Master Jaya. The harness features a 3-leg T-junction linking 8-pin, 3-pin, and dual 2-pin sealed automotive connectors with 15 circuits. Prepared isometric and manufacturing drawings, verified the Bill of Materials (BOM), and established crimp height/width tolerances per automotive standards.

01Industrial Context & Engineering Scope

Automotive wire harnesses are safety-critical assemblies that operate in hostile engine-bay environments characterized by high vibration, extreme temperature cycling (-40°C to +125°C), moisture ingress, and chemical exposure (fuel, oil, coolant). A single poor crimp or unsealed connector terminal can lead to sensor failure or vehicle stoppage.

During my internship in the R&D department at PT Hana Master Jaya, I supported the engineering team in developing the complete drawing package, Bill of Materials (BOM), and crimping quality specifications for a new automotive sensor wire harness (Rev. 0).

Fig 1. Figure 1: Isometric engineering drawing (1:5 scale) showing 3-leg T-geometry, branch lengths, and connector callouts.

02Harness Topology & Mechanical Protection

The harness links multiple engine-bay sensors into the main ECU vehicle backbone:

  • T-Shaped 3-Leg Architecture: Features a primary trunk terminating in an 8-pin sealed female connector, splitting into three branch legs with an intermediate 3-pin connector and two 2-pin waterproof sensor plugs.
  • Circuit Count: Integrates 15 discrete automotive-grade thin-wall cross-linked polyethylene (AVSS/FLRY) insulated conductors with designated color-coding for sensor power, ground, and analog/PWM signals.
  • Harsh Environment Protection: High-impact polypropylene corrugated split conduit protects main wire runs, while flexible vinyl tubing and adhesive-lined heat shrink enclose junctions. Five chassis mounting clips ensure vibration damping and prevent chafing against sheet metal.
Fig 2. Figure 2: Top-view manufacturing layout detailing corrugated conduit, vinyl sleeving, heat-shrink transitions, and mounting clamps.

03BOM Verification & Crimping Specifications

Key technical contributions executed during the design validation phase:

  • Drawing Package Preparation: Supported the senior drafter in producing detailed isometric (1:5), top, and cross-sectional views with precise dimensional tolerances, connector pinouts, and callout tables.
  • BOM Cross-Verification: Cross-checked the comprehensive Bill of Materials against physical assembly prototypes—verifying terminal part numbers, wire gauge sizes (0.5 mm² and 0.85 mm²), silicone wire seals, rubber boots, and conduit diameters.
  • Crimp Height & Width Calculation: Defined critical crimp height (C/H) and crimp width (C/W) specifications for conductor and insulation barrels, referencing manufacturer terminal application tooling guidelines to guarantee gas-tight micro-welds without severed wire strands.
  • Tensile Retention Testing: Established quality pull-test criteria adhering to JIS C 2805 standards, validating that crimped terminal joints withstand required tensile retention forces prior to wire rupture.

04Production Release & Manufacturing Readiness

The documentation package was successfully approved for pre-production trial tooling:

  • Delivered zero-error BOM and wiring schematics adopted into the factory ERP and assembly jig tooling.
  • Standardized crimp validation protocols adopted on the crimping press test benches, ensuring repeatable contact resistance below 5 mΩ.
  • Verified mechanical fitment and clearance on engine mockup jigs with optimal routing slack and strain relief.
