Executive Summary: Engineered an end-to-end autonomous quadrotor system for targeted oil palm pollination. Flight control and mission execution are built on Pymavlink and MAVSDK for deterministic low-latency command streaming. A ROS-based digital twin simulates and validates navigation and control algorithms prior to field deployment. Features real-time YOLOv11 vision on Jetson Nano, an STM32-based pneumatic pollen spray actuator, and full-duplex telemetry.

01Project Scope & Agronomic Challenge

Precision pollination in oil palm plantations presents severe operational constraints: towering canopy heights (8–15 m), erratic tropical gusts, dense leaf clutter, and the demand for milligram-level pollen dosage per flower bunch. Manual pollination is labor-intensive, hazardous, and yields inconsistent fruit bunch set.

To overcome this, I developed an autonomous UAV platform capable of navigating tree rows autonomously, detecting receptive female flower inflorescences in real time, and deploying an electro-pneumatic pollen blast from a stabilized standoff distance of 1.2 meters without human pilot intervention.

02Hardware & Software Architecture

The system is architected as a two-tier heterogeneous computing pipeline:

  • Flight & Actuation Layer (Hard Real-Time): A Pixhawk flight controller running customized PX4 firmware executes primary motor mixing, attitude EKF3 state estimation, and fail-safe return-to-launch (RTL). An auxiliary STM32F103 payload controller interfaces via CAN/UART to pulse the high-pressure solenoid valves and monitor pollen tank reservoir pressure.
  • High-Level Perception & Mission Layer (Soft Real-Time): An onboard Nvidia Jetson Nano handles camera streaming from a Sony IMX477 sensor, executes a TensorRT-optimized YOLOv11 target detection network at 30 FPS, and computes closed-loop visual servoing offsets transmitted over MAVSDK via companion computer UART (/dev/ttyTHS1 at 921600 baud).
  • Digital Twin Validation (ROS/Gazebo SITL): Prior to bench and flight testing, every mission profile was verified in a simulated palm plantation environment using Gazebo SITL and ROS nodes, validating obstacle clearance and trajectory re-planning.

03Engineering Highlights & Mechanical Design

Key design decisions executed across mechanical, electrical, and firmware domains:

  • Vibration-Isolated Payload Bay: Designed custom 3K carbon-fiber plate brackets and SLA-printed TPU dampers in SolidWorks to isolate high-frequency motor vibration (180–220 Hz) from the optical camera and IMU sensors.
  • Pneumatic Dispersion Mechanism: Designed an STM32-actuated micro-solenoid pulse system delivering a 150 ms nitrogen burst, aerosolizing 0.5 g of pollen-talc mixture with uniform cone dispersion.
  • Power Distribution Integrity: Dual isolated power rails (6S 22.2V LiPo for propulsion via 40A BLHeli_32 ESCs; buck-regulated 5V 5A for Jetson Nano and 3.3V for STM32 and optical sensors) preventing inductive back-EMF spikes from corrupting computing logic.
  • Fail-Safe Telemetry Watchdog: Implemented heartbeats at 10 Hz across Jetson, Pixhawk, and ground station. Loss of telemetry triggers instantaneous autonomous loiter and automated RTL after 3.0 seconds.

04Bench & Flight Validation Results

Extensive field and bench testing confirmed robust operational reliability:

  • Achieved sub-50 ms end-to-end command latency from vision target acquisition to pneumatic valve triggering.
  • Maintained position holding precision within ±8 cm in 15 knot wind conditions using fused RTK-GPS and optical flow.
  • YOLOv11 detection achieved 94.2% mAP@0.5 on receptive palm flower clusters under varying tropical sunlight.
