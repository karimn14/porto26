Executive Summary: Designed, fabricated, and integrated an agile 3-wheel holonomic omni-drive mobile soccer robot for the Engineering Physics Laboratory competition. Modeled the lightweight chassis in SolidWorks, developed a custom Kotlin Android app featuring multi-touch virtual joysticks and Wi-Fi UDP command streaming, and wrote the microcontroller C++ firmware for inverse kinematics, closed-loop wheel speed PID control, and dribbler actuation.

01Project Challenge & Leadership Role

The Engineering Physics Laboratory II soccer competition challenged student teams to design an autonomous or teleoperated mobile robot capable of aggressive acceleration, omnidirectional strafing, and ball control in a competitive field environment.

I took the initiative as team lead for system integration—bridging mechanical CAD design, wireless mobile app development, and embedded motor firmware into a cohesive, responsive robotic platform.

Fig 1. Figure 1: Custom Kotlin Android Studio teleoperation interface: dual virtual thumbstick and Wi-Fi UDP packet monitor.

02Holonomic Inverse Kinematics & Motor Control

The mathematical and firmware foundations of the robot:

  • 3-Wheel Omni-Drive Kinematics: Placed three omnidirectional wheels spaced 120 degrees apart. The firmware computes wheel velocity vectors via inverse kinematics: \([v_1, v_2, v_3]^T = M \cdot [v_x, v_y, \omega]^T\), enabling simultaneous translation and rotation in any direction without turning the chassis.
  • Closed-Loop Velocity Regulation: Dual quadrature encoders on each DC gearmotor feed high-frequency timer interrupts, with C++ PID loops stabilizing wheel speeds despite carpet friction changes.
  • Active Dribbler & Kicker: An auxiliary high-RPM brushless roller traps the ball against the chassis frame while a spring-loaded solenoid mechanism executes targeted kicks.
Fig 2. Figure 2: SolidWorks 3D mechanical model: 120-degree omni-wheel geometry, low-CG battery tray, and kicker solenoid bracket.

03Kotlin Android App & SolidWorks Mechanical CAD

Integration between user experience and physical hardware:

  • Low-Latency UDP Telemetry App: Developed a native Android application in Kotlin using Android Studio. Features dual virtual joysticks, transmission rate configuration (up to 50 Hz), and packet checksum validation over Wi-Fi UDP sockets.
  • SolidWorks Lightweight Chassis: Designed a multi-tier acrylic and aluminum plate chassis with a low center of gravity to prevent rollover during rapid acceleration reversals. Incorporated modular battery bays and motor brackets.

04Competition Agility & Responsiveness

Competitive performance on the arena court:

  • Achieved sub-20 ms end-to-end command latency from mobile touchscreen input to motor shaft acceleration.
  • Demonstrated continuous 360-degree vector strafing at speeds up to 1.6 m/s with tight trajectory control.
  • Recognized for superior maneuverability and system integration finish in the lab cohort.
